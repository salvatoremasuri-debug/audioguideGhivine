8. PETROGLYPHS

But what do we know about human presence inside the Grotta del Bue Marino?
The cave is not only a place of extraordinary natural beauty: it is also a site of great archaeological interest. The evidence gathered indicates that the cave was frequented as far back as prehistoric times. Looking at the wall in front of you, you can see a panel of particularly significant rock carvings: approximately twenty anthropomorphic figures incised by hammering, a cupule — a small circular hollow carved into the rock — and two concentric circles with a further cupule at the centre.

According to scholars, these carvings may date back to the Ozieri Culture (4000–3500 BC) or the Filigosa Culture (3000–2500 BC), certainly at a time when the sea level was several metres lower than today and access to the cave most likely took place from the land.

If you look towards the lower right, you will notice an isolated figure with a distinctive "candelabra" structure, equipped with multiple limbs. Just above it, two figures can be made out with oblique lateral appendages. Higher still, there is a figure with arms and legs bent in the same direction.

All the figures display elongated, curved, and inclined limbs, arranged irregularly around the concentric motifs. The most widely accepted interpretation suggests that the scene depicts a group of praying figures arranged around a circular symbol, perhaps connected to religious rituals or to symbolic practices related to nature.

Similar stylised anthropomorphic representations can also be found at other Italian sites, such as the Naquane National Rock Carving Park, the Sas Concas site, and the Grotta del Papa.
The similarities between places that are geographically very distant from one another suggest the existence, in ancient times, of a shared cultural network in which symbols and ritual practices spread among different communities.

What you are looking at is not merely carvings in rock: you are looking at a testimony to the prehistoric spirituality of Sardinia.
At this precise spot, nature and human presence meet, leaving a mark that spans thousands of years of history.
