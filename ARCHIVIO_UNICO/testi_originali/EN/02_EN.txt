2. WHY "BUE MARINO"?

The name Grotta del Bue Marino comes from the presence, until just a few decades ago, of Monachus monachus, the Mediterranean monk seal, known in Sardinian as Su Oe Marinu, the "sea ox" or "sea bull".
The nickname derives from its massive build and the deep sounds it produced inside the coastal cavities: a call that, amplified by the cave walls, was reminiscent of the lowing of an ox.

The Mediterranean monk seal is one of the rarest marine mammals in the world and is a species on the verge of extinction. It is estimated that no more than 500 individuals remain across the entire Mediterranean Sea, concentrated mainly in a few particularly isolated and protected areas, such as specific Greek archipelagos and stretches of the North African coast.
Its presence today is fragmented and precarious, tied to places that remain largely untouched by human activity.

An adult specimen can reach 2.5 metres in length and an average weight of between 250 and 300 kilograms. The body is sturdy, the snout short and rounded, and the eyes large and dark. The coat varies from grey to dark brown.

Until the 1980s, the monk seal lived permanently in the Gulf of Orosei and used these caves as a safe refuge for resting and giving birth.
With the growth of tourism, nautical traffic, and human presence along the coast, the species gradually abandoned the area and eventually disappeared.

It was not, however, an animal particularly appreciated by local fishermen.
Attracted to fish caught in nets, it would tear through them and easily feed on the catch, causing economic damage. This conflict with humans contributed, over time, to its persecution.

Today, its memory lives on in the name of this cave.
Walking through here, amid silence and crystal-clear water, we can imagine a time when this place was the refuge of one of the most fascinating, rare, and vulnerable animals in the Mediterranean.
