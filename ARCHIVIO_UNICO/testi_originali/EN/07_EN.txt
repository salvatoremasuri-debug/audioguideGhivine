7. THE SEAL HALL

Welcome to the Seal Hall, also known as the Seal Beach or the Hall of Two Beaches. This is the final section of the tourist route through the Grotta del Bue Marino.

The name comes from the presence of sand: as you can see, we are no longer walking on an artificial walkway but on the cave's actual floor.
The hall is dedicated to Monachus monachus, the Mediterranean monk seal. It was right here, in the 1970s, that one of the last specimens still present in the Gulf of Orosei was observed: a mother with her pup.

These small internal beaches offered an ideal refuge: a sheltered, silent place, safe from storm swells and — in former times — from human presence as well. They were perfect spaces for resting and nursing the young.

This is not simply a hall of the cave: it is a place that preserves one of the last testimonies of the monk seal's presence in these waters.

Among all the halls on the tourist route of the Grotta del Bue Marino, this is the one that changes most from year to year, precisely because of the active presence of the river.
The sand beneath our feet does not come from the sea, but from the underground river. During winter, depending on the amount of rainfall, the river can increase its flow considerably. At certain times it even covers much of the floor, bringing in new sand or carrying some away, and slightly altering the morphology of the space.

What for us marks the end of the tourist route is, in reality, also the beginning of the speleological section of the system.
From this point onwards, the cave continues and branches out for kilometres, connecting to the Grotta di Su Palu, the Grotta di Su Molente, and the Grotta di Monte Longos, forming the approximately 75 kilometres of explored passages known today.

The speleological section consists of large halls rich in fresh water, wide internal beaches, imposing formations, and numerous sumps: environments completely filled with water that can only be navigated by experienced cavers using scuba equipment and tanks.

The visit ends here.
Before beginning the return journey, you will be directed according to your next destination: Cala Luna, the mini-cruise, or the port of Cala Gonone. Each group will have a specific exit order, so it is essential that you listen carefully to the guides' instructions so as not to risk missing your boat.

Thank you for choosing to visit the Grotta del Bue Marino.
We hope this journey has moved you and allowed you to discover something more about this extraordinary territory and the underground world it conceals.
