6. THE MEETING POINT OF SEA AND RIVER

We are now at the exact point where the sea meets the river, one of the most significant moments on the entire route through the Grotta del Bue Marino.

If you look down beneath you, you will notice a small difference in level: a natural rocky step just a few centimetres high, but enough to separate two different worlds.
In the lower level is salt water, coming from the sea.
In the slightly higher level is fresh water, fed by the underground river.

During the summer months, when rainfall is scarce, the river significantly reduces its flow and tends to stagnate. In these conditions the two bodies of water remain separate, maintaining an almost motionless equilibrium.

During the winter months, on the other hand, the river is fed once again by rain. The flow increases and, already in the low season, if you are lucky, it is right here at this spot that you may observe a small waterfall of fresh water pouring down towards the sea level.

It is a particularly evocative moment: before our very eyes, the process that over millennia has contributed to the formation of these cavities is made clearly visible.

Here we are not merely witnessing the meeting of two bodies of water, but the meeting of two forces: the marine and the fluvial. It is a dynamic equilibrium, which varies with the seasons and which continues, even today, to slowly shape the interior of the mountain.
